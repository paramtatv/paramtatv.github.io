"Naad and Śravaṇa: audio you can decode, read and check"

संस्कृतम् हिन्दी

Sound is the oldest subject of Sanskrit science. The tradition that gave the world a precise phonetics also gave it one word, svara, for both the pitch accent of a spoken syllable and a musical note. At Paramtatva we are bringing audio into SANSOS, our Sanskrit-native operating system, in two parts: Naad (नाद), a lossless decoder that is live today, and Śravaṇa (श्रवण), the programme Naad opens.

Naad: a lossless decoder you can read to the bottom

Naad decodes FLAC, the open lossless audio format, and it is written entirely in Sassembly, our Sanskrit programming language, compiled to 64-bit RISC-V. The whole decoder kernel is 1,907 lines in two files, with 32 public routines, and zero lines of foreign code in the decode path: no C, no C++, no Rust, no hand-written assembly. It uses integer arithmetic only.

You can hear it and read it at https://paramtatv.github.io/naad/. The page plays a full recording of Grieg's In the Hall of the Mountain King, and the decoder source sits one link away in the public repository, https://github.com/paramtatv/naad.

What it has decoded

  • A whole recording, end to end. The 154-second Grieg file on the page, 25,778,747 bytes of FLAC, decodes on the native image to 44,378,214 bytes of 24-bit PCM, and that output's MD5 equals the checksum the original encoder sealed into the file's own header. The proof of correctness comes from someone else's software, not from us.
  • EBU SQAM: 70 of 70. Every recording in the European Broadcasting Union's sound-quality reference set decodes to exactly the MD5 its encoder stored.
  • IETF subset: 57 of 64, which is every mono and stereo file in a corpus built to exercise every encoder choice: block sizes, partition orders, coefficient precisions and bit depths of 8, 16 and 24.
  • IETF faulty set: 11 of 11 deliberately damaged files end in a named status, never an out-of-bounds read or a trap.

Behind those results is a verification ladder. All 64 subframe type codes (39 legal, 25 reserved) and all four header code spaces are enumerated; 10 whole frames are graded; and 17 deliberate mutations of the kernel are each caught by the test built for it.

One stream, one file, every machine

A lossless decoder should give the same bytes everywhere, and Naad shows it. One and the same decoder image ran on four venues: a Mac and a Linux PC (both x86-64), an ARM64 Linux board, and the machine compiled to WebAssembly in a web browser. All four produced one audio digest and one executed-instruction count. That is two instruction sets and a browser agreeing to the instruction.

The cost is measured as well: 2,615 instructions per channel-sample on the path the product runs, decoding a real 24-bit file. On a simple model of a 1 GHz core running one instruction per cycle, that projects to 4.0× real time for the recording on the page.

A taste of the source

Here is how Naad undoes mid/side stereo, copied verbatim from the decoder. The operators are Sanskrit words: योगः (add), वियोगः (subtract), वामसृ (shift left), दक्षिणसृ (shift right), युक् (bitwise and), विकल्प (bitwise or).

        यदि भेदः समम् ३ आदि
            चरः मध्यम् ॱॱ न६४ भवति अ वामसृ १ ।
            चरः अल्पांशः ॱॱ न६४ भवति ब युक् १ ।
            मध्यम् भवति मध्यम् विकल्प अल्पांशः ।
            चरः योगफलम् ॱॱ अ६४ भवति मध्यम् योगः ब ।
            चरः अन्तरम् ॱॱ अ६४ भवति मध्यम् वियोगः ब ।
            वामः अङ्कः इ अन्तः भवति योगफलम् दक्षिणसृ १ ।
            दक्षिणः अङ्कः इ अन्तः भवति अन्तरम् दक्षिणसृ १ ।
        इति

The mid channel is doubled and its lost low bit restored from the side channel. The sum and difference are taken as signed 64-bit values (अ६४), and each is halved back into the left and right channels.

Śravaṇa: where Naad leads

Naad is the first stratum of a larger programme. Śravaṇa aims at bit-exact audio at neural quality. Today the best-sounding audio at low bitrates comes from neural codecs, and floating-point inference does not promise identical output across hardware. Integer-only inference does, and exact integers are the native mode of Sassembly.

We are building Śravaṇa in six strata, each with its own completion condition:

  1. निःशेषम्, lossless: the floor and the proving ground. This is the stratum Naad delivers.
  2. स्वरः, the symbolic layer: voice and music treated as one signal, encoding the control and not only the waveform.
  3. तन्त्रम्: an integer-only neural decoder.
  4. प्रमाणम्, attestation: one stream, one file, every machine.
  5. प्रवाहः, transmission: latency, packet loss and attested delivery.
  6. कोशः, the container: a decoder that travels with the stream it decodes.

Naad's four-venue agreement is the first working instance of the attestation idea. Our direction is to carry the same guarantee upward, from lossless audio to neural quality, so that the best-sounding audio can also be archived and checked.
